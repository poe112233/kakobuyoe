Bags and belts: hardware, stitching, lining

Leather photographs are flattering on every camera. What separates a good bag from a disappointing one is how the metal feels, how the stitches line up, and what the inside looks like.

The three-point inspection

Hardware first. Ask for the clasp, zip puller and any logo plate photographed close up and in daylight. Weight and sharpness of the engraving are what you are judging; colour is easy to fake, machining marks are not.

Stitching second. Look at the corners and where the strap attaches: those are the points where uneven spacing shows up first. Third, the lining. Request a photo of the open interior — this is the part sellers most often skip and the part that reveals material quality.

  • Clasp and zip puller, close-up in daylight
  • Corner stitching and strap attachment points
  • Interior with the bag open: lining material and pocket finishing
  • Full footprint photo against a tape measure for real dimensions
Belts: confirm the buckle size and strap width before ordering, and check the hole spacing. A belt that only just fits the smallest hole will need a punch you may not want to make.

Why dimensions beat names

Bag model names are used loosely, and two listings with the same name can differ by several centimetres. Get a photo of the bag flat against a tape measure in both directions, then measure a bag you already carry. That is the only comparison that holds.
