Kakobuy questions, answered straight

The twenty questions that come up most often, grouped by the stage they belong to. No account needed to read them, and no payment happens on this site.

Getting started

Most first-order confusion is about who does what. The platform buys, the warehouse inspects, the courier delivers, and you pay at three separate moments. Once that division is clear, the rest follows.

What exactly does a buying platform do?
It purchases an item from a Chinese marketplace on your instruction, receives it at its own warehouse, photographs it for your inspection, and then forwards it internationally once you approve and pay the shipping charge. It does not sell the goods itself.
Is this site the official Kakobuy website?
No. kakobuyoe.shop is an independent guide published by Oriental Essence. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or acting on behalf of Kakobuy or any marketplace mentioned here.
Do I need an account here?
No. This site is a static guide. There is no sign-up, no login, no cart and no payment. You read, then follow a link to the source listing.
What is a spreadsheet in this context?
A browsable catalogue of items other buyers have already vetted, usually organised by category and brand. It is a discovery layer: it helps you find candidates, and the actual order happens on the platform you choose.
How many languages does this guide come in?
Four: English, Simplified Chinese, Spanish and Portuguese. The language menu in the header switches all pages, and each language version is linked to the others so search engines can group them correctly.
How often is the item index refreshed?
The catalogue snapshot behind these pages is refreshed on a regular schedule. Item availability, price and QC options change on the marketplace side, so always confirm the current listing before paying.
Can I order for someone else?
Yes, you address the parcel to whoever will receive it. Check the destination country's import rules first: in some places personal imports are treated differently from gifts or commercial shipments.

Ordering, QC and shipping

The two decisions that cause the most trouble are both timing questions: when to inspect, and when to ship. Inspect before the parcel is submitted, and ship before the free storage window closes.

Do I pay the seller directly?
No. You pay the platform, and the platform buys from the seller. That is what gives you a warehouse step and an inspection opportunity in the first place.
Why is the item price so much lower than retail?
Because you are buying the source listing, not a retail-marked-up product: no retail rent, no brand marketing budget, no distributor margin. The trade-off is that you handle the shipping decisions yourself.
What if the warehouse photos show a problem?
Raise it with support before the parcel is submitted and ask for extra photos: another angle, a measurement, the interior. Once the item is in an international shipment, your options narrow sharply.
How long can items stay in the warehouse?
There is normally a free storage window, after which daily charges can apply. The exact period is set by the platform and shown in your account — check it before ordering something with a long lead time.
Can I combine orders into one parcel?
Yes, and it usually lowers the total because the fixed handling cost is paid once. Compare it against splitting, though: two lighter parcels sometimes cost less than one heavy one.
Will I pay customs duty?
It depends on your country's threshold and the declared value. Rules change frequently, so confirm the current position locally. Duty is charged on arrival and is not something the platform can waive.
What happens if a parcel is lost or damaged?
Report it to the platform's support as soon as the tracking shows a problem, with the order number and photos. This is exactly why an accurate declared value matters: cover is usually capped at it.
Can I return an item?
Returns are usually practical only at the warehouse, before international dispatch. After that, return shipping normally costs more than the item, which is why the inspection step deserves real attention.

Costs, links and editorial policy

Being clear about how a guide makes money is part of being trustworthy. So: this site does not sell anything, does not take payment, and does not change the price you pay at any destination.

Does this site earn from the links?
Outbound links lead to the catalogue and to the platform you choose. Regardless of any referral arrangement that may exist, the checks and guidance on these pages are written to help you get the order right, and nothing here changes the price you pay at those destinations.
How is the content reviewed?
Pages carry a review date. Category guides are written from the buyer's side and focus on what is verifiable in photos and measurements rather than on claims that cannot be checked. Where rules or rates can change, the page says so and points you to confirm.
Do you publish prices?
No. Source prices change constantly and quoting a stale figure is worse than quoting none. Instead, the guides explain how cost is built up so you can compare any two options yourself at whatever the current numbers are.
Are the brand names used here affiliated with you?
No. Product and brand names appear for identification only and belong to their respective owners. Their presence does not imply any endorsement, partnership or authenticity claim.
Can I suggest a category or report an error?
Yes — use any of the channels in the footer. Corrections with a screenshot or a link are the fastest to act on, and reviewed pages are updated when something material changes.
