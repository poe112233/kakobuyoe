Watches: read the photos like a caseback

Case diameter and lug-to-lug decide how a watch wears; the movement and the dial close-ups decide whether you will still like it in six months.

Wearability before specification

A 41mm case with long lugs wears far larger than a 41mm case with short ones. Always ask for the lug-to-lug measurement, then compare it against a watch you already wear comfortably. This is the single most useful number in the category and it is almost never in the listing.

Case thickness matters too, especially on chronographs. Measure it against a cuff or shirt sleeve you actually wear.

  • Dial close-up at an angle: date window alignment and index symmetry
  • Caseback and crown: engraving depth, thread quality, seal condition
  • Bracelet or strap end links: fit tolerance against the lugs
  • Lume shot if the listing claims one, plus a timed shot for a quartz sweep

If the seller cannot photograph the movement, assume the listing is describing the dial and nothing more.

Shipping a watch safely

Insist on a box inside a box, and remove the strap from the case if it is a metal bracelet so the links cannot rub the case sides. Declare a realistic value — an underdeclared watch is usually uninsured at the moment you most need the cover.
