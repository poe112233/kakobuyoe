Shipping cost: how the number is actually built

There is no single shipping price, only a price for a given weight on a given route to a given country. Once you can see the three variables, you can price any parcel yourself.

Reviewed 2026-10-14 · Oriental Essence

On this page

  1. Variable one: chargeable weight
  2. Variable two: the route
  3. Variable three: the destination country
  4. Pricing consolidation properly
  5. Five habits that cut the bill

Variable one: chargeable weight

Couriers do not bill what your parcel weighs. They bill the greater of the scale weight and a volumetric weight derived from the parcel's dimensions, using a divisor that varies by carrier and route — commonly 5000 or 6000 when measured in centimetres.

Run the arithmetic once and it stops being abstract. A box of 50×35×20 cm is 35,000 cm³. At a divisor of 6000 that is about 5.8 kg; at 5000 it is 7.0 kg. If the goods inside weigh 1.2 kg, you are paying for 5.8 kg. Empty space is not free; it is the most expensive material in the parcel.

Always ask for the packed dimensions and the packed weight before submitting. Estimating from the product's listing dimensions will be wrong by exactly the amount of packaging the warehouse adds.

Variable two: the route

Two routes to the same country can differ substantially on the same weight, because they differ in mode, handling, transit time and what they will carry. Economy routes are slower and often cheaper per kilo; express routes cost more and buy time.

The trap is a route with a low headline rate per kilo but a high minimum charge. On a 1 kg parcel, the minimum dominates. Always compare the quoted total for your actual weight, never the per-kilo figure.

Variable three: the destination country

The same parcel priced to three countries is three different prices. Beyond freight, the destination determines which routes exist at all, what documentation is needed, and whether duty is collected upfront or on delivery. Some countries treat personal imports generously and others do not; the rules also change, sometimes annually.

Pricing consolidation properly

Consolidating several items into one parcel reduces the fixed handling component, which is why it usually wins. But compare carefully: combining 3 kg and 4 kg into 7 kg may push you past a weight tier where the per-kilo rate jumps, so the combined parcel can cost more than two separate ones.

The reliable method is to ask for two quotes for the same contents — combined and split — and take the lower. It costs one message.

Five habits that cut the bill

  1. Ask for packed dimensions, not product dimensions
  2. Quote two routes for your actual weight and compare totals
  3. Fill chargeable weight with flat, light items rather than heavy ones
  4. Ask whether compression is allowed on your route for soft goods
  5. Check the free storage deadline before ordering a slow item
Rates, divisors, minimum charges and duty thresholds all change. Treat any figure you read as a method, and confirm live numbers with the platform before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my parcel charged at a higher weight than it weighs?
Because volumetric weight exceeds the scale weight. Multiply the three dimensions in centimetres and divide by the carrier's divisor to see the figure you are actually paying on.
Is the cheapest route always the best?
Only if you do not need speed or cover. Check what the route includes: handling quality, tracking, and whether basic cover is bundled or requires a supplement.
Can I avoid duty by declaring less?
It reduces duty in the best case and removes your cover in the worst, because compensation is usually capped at the declared value. That is a bad trade on anything you would mind losing.
Do two lighter parcels ever beat one heavy one?
Yes, when the combined parcel crosses a weight tier or when one route has a ceiling. That is why asking for both quotes is worth the extra message.
