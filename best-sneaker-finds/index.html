Sneaker finds: choosing by silhouette and weight, not hype

Sneakers are the most ordered category and the easiest to get wrong. The good news is that the failure modes are visible in photos, if you know which three angles to ask for.

Reviewed 2026-10-14 · Oriental Essence

On this page

  1. Silhouettes that ship well, and silhouettes that do not
  2. The three angles that reveal a pair
  3. The size check that prevents most returns
  4. What to accept, and what to send back

Silhouettes that ship well, and silhouettes that do not

A pair's shipping cost is set by its packed volume, not by its price. Low-profile trainers and slides pack flat and fill a parcel cheaply. Chunky runners, thick-soled boots and anything with a large box can multiply the chargeable weight of a parcel by several times.

A practical strategy for a first parcel: one pair you actually want, plus two or three flat items from other categories. That combination fills chargeable weight efficiently, which is what makes freight feel reasonable.

StylePacked behaviourVerdict for a first order
Low-profile trainersPacks flat, cheap per kiloIdeal companion item
Running shoesCompact and light, some volume in the heelGood value per kilo
Chunky runners / skateSubstantial volume, occasional box bulkFine, but not as your only item
BootsHeaviest per pair and highest volumetric weightPlan a whole parcel around them

The three angles that reveal a pair

  1. Heel and outsole at three-quarters — this is where silhouettes diverge first, because the heel counter shape and the outsole tread are hard to copy cleanly
  2. Midsole in profile — look at the seam line between upper and sole for consistency along its length
  3. Toe box from a low angle — pattern alignment and the shape of the toe cap
Front-on photos are the least useful angle for a sneaker and the most common in listings. Always ask for the three-quarter view.

The size check that prevents most returns

Ask for the insole measured with a ruler, in centimetres, photographed flat. Not the box label, not a US letter — the actual measurement. Then compare it with the insole of a pair you already wear comfortably: take the insole out and measure it the same way.

Two centimetres of difference in that number is a different fit, regardless of what the tag says. This single request prevents more disappointment than any size chart can.

What to accept, and what to send back

  • Accept: minor adhesive residue on a midsole, small creases from packing, a slightly dented box
  • Escalate: wrong colourway, inconsistent sizing across the pair, a structural gap at the sole junction, obvious pattern misalignment
  • Escalate immediately: any pair where the two shoes are visibly different in shape or measurement
Never submit a parcel containing a pair you intend to dispute. After international dispatch, returning a pair costs more than most pairs are worth.

Frequently asked questions

Are these pairs authentic?
This site makes no authenticity claim about any item, and warehouse photos cannot establish it. What they can do is confirm that the item that arrived matches what you ordered.
Should I keep the box?
Only if you want the box. Boxes add volume that is billed by volumetric weight, so for a single pair, leaving it behind is often the cheaper choice.
How many pairs fit in a sensible parcel?
Two to three flat pairs plus a couple of light garments is a common efficient combination. Chunky styles should be counted as though they occupied twice the space.
What if the size is right but the fit is not?
That is a shape difference, not a size problem, and it is not a valid dispute. It is why the insole measurement, not the label, is the number to compare.
