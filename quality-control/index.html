The QC checklist: reading warehouse photos properly

Warehouse photos are taken quickly and in fixed positions. Knowing what each angle can and cannot tell you is the difference between catching a problem and finding it after the parcel arrives.

Reviewed 2026-10-14

On this page

  1. What warehouse photos are for
  2. The five-point check
  3. Category-specific checks
  4. How to request extra photos
  5. When to accept, and when to escalate

What warehouse photos are for

They confirm that the item that reached the warehouse is the item you ordered, and that it is in the condition the seller claimed. They are not an authentication service and they do not test function: a watch is photographed, not timed; a jacket is measured, not worn.

That distinction matters because it tells you what to escalate. If the photo shows a different colourway, that is a clear discrepancy. If it shows a faint scuff that might be a lighting artefact, ask for a second photo under different light before deciding.

Ask for photos at full resolution. Thumbnails hide exactly the details — edge finishing, small marks, thread tension — that you are looking for.

The five-point check

Run the same five checks in the same order every time. Consistency is what turns a glance at a photo into an actual inspection.

  • Identity — does the colour, model and variant in the photo match the order line?
  • Size — is there a size tag or a measurement visible, and does it match the variant you chose?
  • Quantity — are all units present, and are pairs actually matching pairs?
  • Condition — marks, creases, glue residue, scuffs. Ask about anything you are unsure of.
  • Completeness — box, dust bag, spare links, tags: only if the listing promised them.

How to request extra photos

Write the request as a numbered list and name the angle you want, the thing you are checking, and why. Vague requests get vague photos. A useful request looks like: “1) the size tag inside the shoe, photographed flat; 2) the insole measured with a ruler, in centimetres; 3) the left shoe from the heel, to check the midsole line.”

Do this before submitting the parcel. Once the item is inside an international shipment, a request has to travel back through the warehouse, which costs days you may not have.

When to accept, and when to escalate

Accept minor variations that the listing did not promise against: small marks inside a sole, a box that is slightly dented. Escalate anything that changes what you actually received: wrong variant, wrong size, missing items, or damage that affects use.

Know what is not a valid complaint, so you do not waste the storage window: a colour that looks different under warehouse lighting, a scent of new material, or packaging that is not the retail box when the listing never offered one.

Never ship an item you intend to dispute. Once it is on an international route, a return is usually more expensive than the item.
How many photos should I expect?
It varies by platform and item. Usually a set of standard angles; additional angles are available on request during the storage window.
Do photos prove authenticity?
No. They show condition and identity. Authenticity is a separate question and generally requires an expert inspection service.
Can I reject an item at the warehouse?
Usually you can return it to the seller within the storage window, with the domestic leg typically not refunded. Check the platform's current terms before ordering.
What if the item never arrives at the warehouse?
Contact support with the order number. A seller who never ships is normally refunded, but it takes the domestic transit window plus the platform's own processing time.
Should I ask for measurements on every order?
On anything where fit matters — shoes, clothing, rings — yes. It is a one-line request and it removes the most common source of disappointment.
